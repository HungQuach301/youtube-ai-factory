# Business Operating Model

**State:** `ACTIVE_NORMATIVE`
**Policy:** `FACTORY_BUSINESS_OPERATING_MODEL_V1`
**Effective:** 2026-08-24

## Purpose and business outcome

YouTube AI Factory is a multi-channel operating system that converts evidence-backed market opportunities into differentiated, rights-safe, release-ready videos and learns from exact publication outcomes. The business objective is repeatable quality and unit economics, not maximum generation volume.

## Human/AI operating modes

The default target is minimum owner attention with maximum evidence. Modes progress only through stored qualification:

1. `AI_SHADOW` — human decision; AI calibration.
2. `AI_PRIMARY_HUMAN_SAMPLE` — AI first; human reviews escalations, new-format firsts and samples.
3. `AI_AUTONOMOUS_EXCEPTION_ONLY` — AI accepts/rejects routine qualified work; risk-based audit.
4. `FULL_AUTOPILOT_RELEASE_READY` — AI may create release-ready receipts; publication remains separate.

## Exception-management workflow

```text
Detect -> classify -> prove exact evidence -> assign one root owner
-> contain affected scope -> decide repair/escalate/stop
-> one bounded append-only revision -> independent recheck
-> close with recurrence control or escalate architecture incident
```

Exceptions are classified as content defect, assurance incomplete, rights/legal ambiguity, provider/runtime incident, budget/capacity breach, novel brand risk or publication exception. The exception inbox shows authority, evidence, cost exposure and exact next action. QA is not a generic repair owner.

## Unit economics

Each Factory, channel, format, video and capability view records:

- planned, reserved, actual and billing-verified cost;
- provider, compute, storage, review and remediation cost;
- latency, queue wait, first-pass yield and accepted-output yield;
- cost per minute, accepted video, qualified capability and release-ready master;
- expected revenue/value range and contribution margin after rights/production cost;
- failed-spend, unknown-spend reserve and reuse savings without double counting.

Scale decisions use accepted-output cost and yield, never request volume or estimated cost alone.

## Portfolio budget and capacity arbitration

Portfolio policy allocates channel envelopes, reserved contingency and shared-capability capacity. Arbitration order is: safety/rights obligations, active exact-master completion, time-sensitive committed content, qualified experiments, then discretionary backlog. A channel cannot consume another channel's reserved budget or lease. Unknown spend and active requests reduce available capacity until reconciled.

## Qualification, scale, revoke and rollback

Capability promotion requires qualified dependencies, first-pass yield, no critical false-clean, acceptable latency/cost and an evidence minimum across independent runs. Channel/format scale also requires stable Visual/Voice DNA, no novel risk and a rollback version.

Revoke or rollback occurs when rights/terms change, provider/model/rubric drift makes qualification stale, P0/P1 escapes, cost/latency breaches persist, incident evidence is incomplete or performance degrades beyond the active control band. Rollback selects a prior version for future work; it never rewrites past artifacts or receipts.

# Hidden Systems Visual DNA and R22 Blueprint

**State:** `ACTIVE_CHANNEL_STANDARD__R22_DESIGN_ONLY`

**Channel:** `Hidden Systems Behind Money`
**Market/language:** United States / English `en-US`

## Channel promise

Reveal the invisible rules, actors, data, obligations and failure modes behind everyday money in a way that feels documentary, cinematic, precise and understandable on a phone.

The channel never presents itself as AI-generated. It avoids banking-internal language, Vietnamese audience text, generic corporate slides, unexplained diagrams and abstract motion that does not change meaning.

## Audience

Curious US adults who use financial products but do not work inside payment or banking operations. They want to understand what really happens, who bears risk, where money or data moves, why delays occur and what changes their outcome.

## Visual identity

- Documentary reality anchors establish human stakes and physical context.
- Dark neutral environments with controlled electric cyan, warm amber and risk red accents.
- High-contrast, mobile-first type with progressive disclosure and minimal simultaneous labels.
- Mechanisms use phase-distinct diagrams, ledgers, flows, maps and timelines.
- Motion reveals causal state; camera drift or decorative particles are insufficient.
- The visual payoff completes the promise in observable pixels, not narration alone.

## Treatment palette

| Treatment | Channel role | Signature use |
|---|---|---|
| Real-world macro | Human/merchant/object consequence | Tap, terminal, receipt, package, clock, queue, closed/open state |
| System diagram | Hidden actors and state | Only when node/edge changes are the story |
| Animated ledger | Obligations and balances | Current entries appear only when activated |
| Sankey/data flow | Quantity and direction | One conserved flow, not a generic repeated path |
| Network map | Counterparty or obligation structure | Spatially distinct from geographic maps |
| Geographic map | Cross-border/jurisdiction route | Verified geography and route timing |
| Exception timeline | Delay, reversal, timeout and escalation | Irregular timing and branch consequences |
| Comparison chart | Scale, cost, probability or change | Exact source, units, date and baseline |
| UI/data proof | Controlled system state | Only exact, readable, non-invented evidence |
| Hybrid handoff | Reality to mechanism/proof | Match cut on the same object, signal or value |

## Prohibited patterns

- persistent slide/card template;
- headline band plus static infographic as the default frame;
- one diagram topology relabelled across phases;
- generic stock footage with no claim connection;
- fake dashboards or numbers generated as imagery;
- small low-contrast labels, production residue or debug text;
- future state visible before its event;
- money and data conflated in one undifferentiated path;
- multiple narrator identities or Vietnamese audience text;
- visually unresolved payoff.

## Composition policy

- Prefer one focal causal event per frame.
- Keep the critical label set small enough for mobile viewing; reveal detail sequentially.
- Use safe margins and preserve arrow/route corridors.
- Distinguish actors by stable role coding, not by color alone.
- Support color-blind viewers through shape, label and motion redundancy.
- Rotate treatment families because the evidence job changes, not to satisfy a cosmetic quota.
- No treatment may dominate a sequence without meaningful internal state progression and a Blueprint exception.

## Sound and voice

- One stable en-US narrator identity.
- Conversational authority, controlled pace and clear financial terminology.
- Pronunciation dictionary and voice settings are versioned at Channel level.
- Music supports tension, discovery and payoff without masking narration.
- SFX mark meaningful system events; they do not fill silence mechanically.
- Exact full-duration mix must pass loudness, peak, seam, voice-consistency and audio-native QA.

### R22 repair assertions

1. Merchant settlement and receipt complete visibly before the final takeaway.
2. Authorization is a live request/decision network, not a Sankey or ledger slide.
3. Clearing is a batched obligation/netting mechanism with explicit temporal progression.
4. Settlement is an active ledger/value transfer with only current entries visible.
5. Exception handling uses a branching timeline and consequence, not the normal-path topology.
6. Labels are fewer, larger, high-contrast and mobile-safe.
7. Reality anchors hand off semantically to mechanisms and back to human consequence.
8. Repeated-layout fingerprints and persistent slide grammar fail deterministic preflight.

## R22 Blueprint outline

| Sequence | Viewer question | Reality | Mechanism | Proof/payoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Did the tap already move money? | Customer tap and merchant terminal | Signal leaves the terminal | Receipt state remains unresolved |
| Authorization | Who says yes? | Waiting customer/merchant | Distinct request-response network | Approval is permission, not settlement |
| Clearing | What happens after approval? | Merchant closes day/batch | Batched obligations/netting | Parties owe exact values |
| Settlement | When does value move? | Merchant waiting for funds | Ledger and reserve movement | Merchant balance/receipt visibly completes |
| Exception | Why can it fail or reverse? | Delay/decline consequence | Branch timeline | Root cause and resolution path |
| Payoff | What did the tap actually trigger? | Return to merchant/customer | Compact phase-distinct recap | Complete promise and takeaway |

Exact durations and shot counts are compiled from narration and the canonical timebase. This outline is not an asset list or dispatch authorization.
